Here is the brutal, pixel-level cohesion scorecard for "Algorithmia: The Path of Logic." Based on the stated design intent, the game is currently suffering from severe identity issues, acting as a Frankenstein of different resolutions, palettes, and UI paradigms.

### == PART A — COHESION SCORECARD ==

**A1. PALETTE COHERENCE**
**Score: 2**
**Justification:** The game swings wildly between a dark synth-wave palette (Prologue), sickly split-pea soup greens (Array Plains), and heavily saturated blues (Twin Rivers). The promised "Game Boy 4-color family" intent was completely abandoned outside of a few UI elements. They do not share a believable color world.
**Worst Offender:** `01-menu.png` vs `02-prologue-region-card.png`
**Quote:** "The lush, full-color painterly greens and browns of the menu's floating islands completely betray the harsh, limited dark-teal tech palette established immediately after in the Prologue overworld. It's visual false advertising."

**A2. PIXEL DENSITY CONSISTENCY**
**Score: 1**
**Justification:** This is the single biggest failure of the art direction. Assets are composited together with wildly different native resolutions, instantly shattering the retro illusion. It looks like an amateur asset flip.
**Worst Offender:** `14-professor-node-intro-clean.png`
**Quote:** "The player sprite is drawn with huge, chunky 3x3 pixel blocks for eyes, while Professor Node standing right next to him is drawn with ultra-fine 1x1 pixel details on his goggles and lab coat. They belong on different console generations."

**A3. CHARACTER-VS-WORLD SCALE**
**Score: 2**
**Justification:** The player character feels like a sticker floating across the screen. In the farming and river regions, NPCs and props are gargantuan compared to the environment and the player.
**Worst Offender:** `13-array-plains-marker-panel.png`
**Quote:** "The Village Elder on the left is a towering behemoth compared to the player, and his perspective is drawn almost straight-on, completely clashing with the strict top-down 3/4 grid of the farm dirt below him."

**A4. UI LANGUAGE CONSISTENCY**
**Score: 2**
**Justification:** The core tech UI (cyan/dark teal, sharp pixel fonts) is strong, but the puzzle scenes inexplicably introduce skeuomorphic elements that clash horribly with the overarching sci-fi/tech theme.
**Worst Offender:** `17-ap1-sorting-shed-layout.png`
**Quote:** "The puzzle instruction panel uses rounded, off-white parchment with drop shadows and a modern smooth font ('A Tile Worker waits'), slapped directly underneath a harsh cyan/black tech HUD. It's a total genre collision."

**A5. PUZZLE-VS-OVERWORLD TRANSITIONS**
**Score: 1**
**Justification:** The "deliberate style rupture" only happens in the Prologue. In Array Plains and Twin Rivers, there is no rupture—just a cheap darkening overlay and floating UI boxes placed over the overworld.
**Worst Offender:** `17-ap1-sorting-shed-layout.png`
**Quote:** "Instead of transporting the player to a 'modern game' abstract space like the Prologue did, this scene lazily darkens the overworld farm background and floats wooden boxes over it. It failed the design intent completely."

**A6. BOSS / GATEWAY MOMENT WEIGHT**
**Score: 2**
**Justification:** The boss arenas exist, but they lack distinct character presence or thematic dread. They feel like slightly fancier puzzle backgrounds rather than narrative climaxes.
**Worst Offender:** `12a-boss-sentinel-layout.png`
**Quote:** "This 'Boss Sentinel' layout is visually indistinguishable from a standard tutorial puzzle like 04-p0-1. There is no central antagonist entity, no arena shift, and no atmospheric weight to signify a climax."

**A7. TYPOGRAPHY + READABILITY**
**Score: 3**
**Justification:** Most of the pixel fonts in the tech UI are crisp, but there are glaring violations of the "no AA" rule, and font styles are mixed recklessly in the mid-game.
**Worst Offender:** `01-menu.png`
**Quote:** "The bottom subtitle 'A world of algorithms awaits' is rendered in a muddy, dark-purple anti-aliased font against a brown background. It is illegible and blatantly violates the hard 1px edge constraint."

**A8. LIGHTING / ATMOSPHERE**
**Score: 2**
**Justification:** The overworlds are mostly flat, but random assets introduce soft gradients or inconsistent directional lighting that ruins the pixel-art crispness.
**Worst Offender:** `21-shuffler-domain-layout.png`
**Quote:** "The perimeter of the arena is smothered in a soft, smooth, airbrushed purple glow/vignette. It looks like a default Photoshop soft brush, completely ignoring the 'no smooth gradients' mandate."

### == PART B — THE BIG QUESTIONS ==

**B1.** If a player saw the menu (`01`), they would expect a high-fantasy isometric city-builder or tactics game. If they then jumped to the sleek vector space of `12a` or the flat top-down grimy pixels of `21`, they would confidently say they were looking at three completely different games. The menu is lying about the game's actual perspective and aesthetic.

**B2.** In Array Plains, the puzzles do NOT feel like a stylistic rupture into a "modern game." They just feel like the overworld with an intrusive, ugly UI layer drawn on top. Compare `08` to `17-20`: it's the exact same background, just darkened. The "rupture" constraint was abandoned here; it looks like a developer debug menu opened over the gameplay.

**B3.** Twin Rivers suffers the exact same fatal flaw. `22` vs `23-26` shows no stylistic rupture. It's just the top-down river tileset with a parchment UI box spawned in the center. It feels like a UI mock-up overlay, not a distinct puzzle dimension.

**B4.** The bosses do not feel like a coherent family. `12a` (Sentinel) is an abstract network of vector nodes in deep space. `21` (Shuffler) is a haunted stone circle in a forest. `27` (Mirror Serpent) is a dual-biome stone circle. They lack a unifying visual antagonist language (e.g., a shared "glitch" color, specific geometry, or corruption texture).

**B5.** 
*   **Weakest: Array Plains.** The color palette is a flat, unappealing mix of muddy greens; the NPC scale is broken (giant Elder); and the puzzle UI introduces out-of-place wooden/parchment skeuomorphism. 
*   **Strongest: Prologue.** It actually commits to the dark tech/ruins atmosphere, and its transition from overworld (`03`) to abstract vector puzzle (`04`) perfectly nails the requested "deliberate style rupture."

### == PART D — SURPRISES ==

**D1. Style Targets:**
*   **`04-p0-1-layout.png`:** The vector puzzles in the prologue are sleek, modern, and perfectly execute the "rupture" intent. 
*   **`08c-codex-hotkey.png`:** The Codex UI is a masterclass. It is crisp, highly readable, and perfectly executes the 4-color dark-teal tech vibe. Use this as the UI bible.

**D2. The Biggest "AI Tell":**
*   **Schizophrenic pixel density within a single composition.** Backgrounds, props, and characters were clearly generated in isolation at different resolutions, then pasted together without snapping to a master grid or enforcing a uniform "pixel size." It is the fastest way to break immersion in a retro game.
